# Approval gates for external actions

AI-assisted workflows often mix reversible preparation with actions that affect customers, communities, or public systems. Treat the boundary between those phases as an explicit approval gate.

## Actions that deserve a gate

Common examples include:

- publishing a post, comment, review, or directory listing;
- sending email or direct messages;
- creating an account or accepting legal terms;
- purchasing a plan or starting a paid trial;
- deleting cloud data or changing access controls.

The approval request should identify the destination, the content or change, and the account that will perform it. “Ready to publish this Wagglet listing on Example Directory from the company account” is clearer than “Continue?”

## Prepare first, approve once

Gather facts, draft copy, upload non-sensitive assets, and validate required fields before asking. When several actions are well defined, group them into one concise review packet. Do not request approval so early that the final content is still unknown.

## Verify after the action

Approval permits the action; it does not prove the outcome. After submission, check the public result, confirm the intended link or data is present, and distinguish “pending review” from “live.” Store the resulting URL and status with the task.
